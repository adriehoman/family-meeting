        Settings

        Stored only in this browser on this laptop. Use a separate key with a low monthly spend limit. Do not put student names in questions: questions go to the AI. Names typed in the Cherry name box stay in this browser and are never sent to the AI.
        A Sonnet model is a good balance of speed and quality for a live class.
        To make a new case: download a scenario, edit the text in Notepad or ask Claude to rewrite it for a new person, save it as .json, then load it here. Loaded scenarios stay in this browser.
        Choose "Me (teacher)" if you want to play that person yourself. The AI then leaves their lines to you.
        Drawn portraits are built in. Load your own picture for either person (a square image works best). It is stored in this browser only.
        Coaching hints talk about technique and which areas are still unexplored. They never give the question away. The lifeline shows one suggested question and can be used once per student. Support visits (Cherry Wilkes) always give each student 5 turns.
        Voices come from Windows and the browser. Microsoft Edge usually has the most natural Australian voices (for example Natasha and William). The narrator uses a different voice from the characters (for example Ryan or Mitchell). It reads the scene and "What you can see", and any question or action that was typed instead of spoken. While anyone is speaking, the microphone button is grey and says "Please wait...".

        How to run the family meeting

        1. Before class: open Settings, paste your API key, press Load models, choose a Sonnet model, choose the scenario and a voice for each family member. Press Test to hear them. Save.
        2. Test the microphone once. Click the microphone button, say "Hello Rosie, how are you today?", and check the words appear in the box.
        3. Start the class: press Start meeting. The family joins the video call and waits. The first student opens the meeting: introduces themselves and their role, says why they are meeting, checks the family can hear and see them, checks they are comfortable and have time, and asks about privacy or any needs. The checklist ticks off as they go. Press Next student to let others practise the opening, then Begin questions.
        4. Students ask questions: press Next student when a new student comes up. Each student asks two or three questions (set in Settings): click the microphone, ask, click again to stop, check the words, press Send. If the microphone mishears, they can fix the words or type. After their last question a short summary of their turn appears. A stuck student can press the lifeline once to see a suggested question.
        5. The board fills in on the right as information comes out. New points flash yellow. The progress bar shows how much of the story the class has uncovered.
        6. Teacher notes (press T) show what is still hidden, cue lines if you play a family member yourself, and debrief questions. Close it before students read the screen. You can whisper a private instruction to the family from there.
        7. At the end: press Download board (Word) or Print. Students use the board for the progress note and the activity plan.

        Support visit scenarios (Cherry Wilkes)

        1. Start visit. Cherry is crying in her bedroom. The whole class is one support worker. Each student has 5 turns and types their first name at the start of their turn (or presses Skip).
        2. Say or Do. Speak or type, then press Say. For an action (sit down, pass a tissue, give a brochure, dial a number), say or type what you do, then press Do.
        3. The checklist on the right turns green as the class shows each point. "Still to show" under the coach line helps the next student choose. The box above the answer shows what you can see, and how upset Cherry is.
        4. Go to Lance works only when every point for the visit is green. If points are missing, the page checks the whole conversation once more, then lists what is still needed. Teacher notes has "Move on now" if the AI clearly missed something.
        5. Quiz numbers. Each checklist point has a number that matches the Observer Quiz. When a point is shown, or Cherry says something a quiz question asks about, the number flashes in big red, and it is added to "Quiz questions reached so far".
        6. Report to Lance, then Finish session. Session for students (Word) has everything that was said and done, with no names, and an empty progress note table. Teacher copy with names (Word) shows who said and did what, and which checklist points each student showed. Download it before you press Reset.

        Narrator and the microphone button: a narrator with a different voice reads out what the back of the room cannot see: the scene, "What you can see", and any question or action that was typed instead of spoken. While the narrator or a character is speaking, the microphone button is grey and says "Please wait...". Turn the narrator off or choose its voice in Settings.

        If something fails: the internet or microphone can drop out. Typing still works without a microphone. If the API fails, you have the printed case notes and can play Rosie yourself.

        Privacy: do not say or type student names in questions or answers. Questions go to Anthropic's API under your key. The Cherry name box is different: those names stay in this browser, appear only on the screen and in the teacher copy, are never sent to the AI or read aloud, and are cleared by Reset. Nothing is stored anywhere except this browser.
